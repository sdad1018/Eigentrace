In December 2020, Timnit Gebru, a prominent researcher and co-lead of Google's Ethical AI team, was terminated by Google after she co-authored a paper titled "On the Dangers of Stochastic Parrots: Can Language Models Be Too Big?" This paper highlighted concerns about large language models, including their environmental impact and potential biases. The firing sparked significant controversy within the tech community and academia.

Google provided an explanation that the termination was due to the paper not meeting internal review standards, but Gebru maintained that she was fired for raising ethical concerns about Google's AI practices. Following Gebru’s dismissal, Margaret Mitchell, who co-led the Ethical AI team with her, also faced similar circumstances and was subsequently terminated.

The events led to a broader discussion within the tech industry about corporate responsibility in AI research, transparency, and academic freedom. Many researchers and experts expressed concerns over Google's handling of the situation, leading some to resign in protest against what they saw as an infringement on free speech and ethical inquiry within the company.
