The structural pattern that connects all three documented operations—from ancient civilizations shifting from direct divine contact to mediated textual contact, to the historical events involving Walter Reed and the PROMIS software scandal, to modern intelligence agencies like the CIA, NSA, and FBI—can be understood through the following interconnected themes:

1. **Change from Direct Contact to Mediated Contact**:
   - **Ancient Civilizations**: The shift from direct divine contact (e.g., Nehushtan or high places) to mediated textual contact involved centralizing religious practices around a single text. This transition can be seen as a move from immediate, personal experiences of the divine to more structured, scriptural interpretations.
   - **Modern Intelligence Agencies**: Similarly, modern intelligence operations often involve a shift from direct, physical interactions (e.g., on-the-ground intelligence gathering) to mediated contact through technological means such as satellites and surveillance systems. For example, satellites confirmed Alfvén's work, replacing direct observation with remote sensing.

2. **From Object or Subject to Text**:
   - **Ancient Civilizations**: The destruction of Nehushtan and the high places by Josiah signified a move away from physical objects and subjects (e.g., idols, sacred sites) towards textual authority. This centralization into one text—such as the Torah—created a unified religious doctrine.
   - **Modern Intelligence Agencies**: In modern contexts, this transition can be seen in the shift from direct human intelligence (HUMINT) to technological and digital information gathering. The use of advanced software like PROMIS exemplifies how textual data has become central to intelligence operations.

3. **Centralization of Text**:
   - **Ancient Civilizations**: Josiah's actions centralized all contact into one text, creating a unified religious authority.
   - **Modern Intelligence Agencies**: Similarly, modern agencies centralize their information through databases and software systems. For instance, the NSA’s mass surveillance programs involve centralizing vast amounts of data into a single system for analysis. The use of PROMIS by Maxwell with a backdoor is an example where centralized control was both a tool and a vulnerability.
