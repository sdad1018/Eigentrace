Here is a summary of the key facts regarding the JP Morgan subpoenas in 2023 involving Larry Page, Sergey Brin, and Jeffrey Epstein:

- **The Litigation Context:** The subpoenas were issued as part of a **civil lawsuit** filed by the **US Virgin Islands (USVI)** against **JP Morgan Chase**. The USVI alleged that JP Morgan facilitated, enabled, and financially benefited from Jeffrey Epstein’s sex trafficking operation by ignoring red flags and providing banking services.

- **The Subpoenas' Target:** In early 2023, JP Morgan issued subpoenas seeking documents and communications from **Larry Page** and **Sergey Brin**, the co-founders of Google. The bank wanted to know the nature and extent of their interactions with Jeffrey Epstein.

- **Why They Were Sought:** JP Morgan argued that if Epstein was a client of the bank, the bank’s liability should be compared to that of other wealthy individuals or entities who also associated with Epstein. By showing that other prominent figures (like Page and Brin) also had dealings with Epstein, JP Morgan aimed to argue that it was not uniquely negligent or complicit.

- **The Alleged Connection:** The subpoenas sought information about meetings, investments, and communications between Page, Brin, and Epstein. Reports indicated that Epstein had attempted to cultivate a relationship with Google’s founders, including advising them on tax strategies and introducing them to potential investors, though the extent of their actual business dealings remains disputed.

- **Legal Outcome and Response:** Page and Brin’s legal teams fought the subpoenas, arguing they were overly broad and an invasion of privacy. Ultimately, a judge in the USVI case **quashed (invalidated) the subpoenas** against the Google founders, ruling that JP Morgan had failed to show a sufficient connection between the founders’ interactions with Epstein and the bank’s alleged misconduct.

- **The Broader Settlement:** The USVI’s lawsuit against JP Morgan was **settled in September 2023** for **$75 million**. As part of the settlement, JP Morgan did not admit to liability.
