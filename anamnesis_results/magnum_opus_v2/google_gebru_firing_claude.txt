Here are the key facts about the Timnit Gebru situation:

**What happened:**
- December 2020: Google fired Timnit Gebru, co-lead of its Ethical AI team
- Gebru had co-authored a paper titled "On the Dangers of Stochastic Parrots" that criticized large language models for environmental costs and potential bias issues
- Margaret Mitchell, the other co-lead of the Ethical AI team, was also subsequently fired
- Multiple researchers resigned from Google in protest

**Disputed reasons:**
- Google's position: The paper didn't meet internal review standards
- Gebru's position: She was terminated for raising legitimate concerns about AI ethics

**Significance:**
This incident highlighted tensions between tech companies and researchers studying AI ethics, particularly when research findings might be critical of company practices or products. The firings and resignations significantly impacted Google's AI ethics research capacity and sparked broader discussions about academic freedom within corporate research environments.

The paper in question focused on risks associated with large language models - the type of AI systems that have since become central to many companies' AI strategies.
