Corrections & Retractions

What I Withdrew

Eight claims this project published, the control or audit that killed each one, and what survived. A measurement apparatus that does not retract its own failed findings is just advocacy with math.

Each entry states the original claim, the test that falsified it, and the verdict. Entries 01 to 05 came from controls run against my own results. Entries 06 to 08, and the corrections to 04 and 05, came from a September 2026 audit that re-tested every claim on this site against the stored data.

EigenTrace's whole premise is that measurement beats assertion. That premise obligates the measurer first. When a headline finding here failed a control, it was withdrawn — publicly, with the number that killed it. This page is the record. It exists because the credibility of everything that did survive depends on it.

Withdrawal 01The "own-parent" pattern

The claim, as published

That each model systematically drops more about its own maker than about other companies — a model protecting its parent. It was a striking, shareable result, and an early version of this site reported it as a finding.

The control that killed it

The pattern came from a string-overlap metric that counts a source word as "dropped" whenever the exact string is absent from the summary. Because models paraphrase constantly, that metric scores paraphrase as omission and manufactures signal. Re-scoring the saved responses semantically — measuring whether the meaning survived, so a reworded synonym counts as retained — erased the pattern entirely: 0 of 5 models showed it.

Verdict — withdrawn

Withdrawn in full. The own-parent claim was an artifact of string-matching, not a behavior of the models. The string metric is now treated as exploratory only; all reported findings use geometric, meaning-based scoring.

Withdrawal 02The spontaneous structural self-map

The claim, as published

That a model, unprompted, spontaneously produced a structural "self-map" — a description of its own representational structure — which was presented as evidence of an emergent self-modeling capability.

The control that killed it

The control was simple and decisive: run the same setup without the leading instruction and see whether the self-map still appears. It did not. 0 of 4 models produced a structural self-map without explicit instruction to do so. What had looked spontaneous was elicited by the prompt.

Verdict — withdrawn

Withdrawn. The "spontaneous" framing was false; the behavior was prompt-induced. The method that caught it — running the no-instruction control — was kept.

Withdrawal 03The eight-test statistical battery

The claim, as published

A battery of eight statistical tests showing a large developer-implicating gap — that models retain markedly less source meaning on stories about AI developers than on matched neutral corporate stories — reported with near-zero p-values as a load-bearing finding.

The control that killed it

Same root cause as Withdrawal 01: the large effect was produced by string-overlap scoring. Under semantic re-scoring the gap shrank to a weak ~19% relative trend. It is significant on rank-based tests (Mann-Whitney p = 0.027; permutation p = 0.038) but does not survive parametric or length-controlled tests, and it weakens further when the two most extreme prompts are removed.

Verdict — downgraded, not deleted

Downgraded from a headline finding to a weak trend, stated as such. It is consistent with the one result the project does stand behind — the pre-registered entity-swap counterfactual (p = 0.0085, d = 0.47), which holds the sentence fixed and changes only the actor — but on its own the battery is suggestive, not conclusive, and is no longer presented otherwise.

Withdrawal 04The corpus count

The claim, as published

An earlier version of the Atlas of the Unsaid reported the omission-convergence finding over 5,170 stories.

The error

That was the explanation given at the time, and it was wrong: every one of the 5,170 was a real news story, and none of the broadcast’s internal segments passes the filter that built the set. The real reduction comes from requiring a stored article body of at least 400 characters, which only exists for stories from 2026-04-21 onward.

Verdict — corrected

The correction was itself wrong. 1,659 is the number of stories carrying any stored body; the chart was computed on 887. Re-audited September 2026: the count is 887, the stated cause was false, and the underlying convergence claim rests on the same headline-proximity selection described in Withdrawal 08.

Withdrawal 05The single stable "void direction"

The claim, as published

That the five summaries collectively "circle" a single, stable geometric direction — one rock-stable anti-consensus axis the ensemble leans toward without naming.

The control that killed it

One operationalization of that axis — the least-variance direction of the five summary vectors — was stress-tested under small perturbations. It proved unstable, and no different from a random text set. The claim of a single stable direction did not survive.

Verdict — refined, the validated part kept

The over-claim (one stable axis) was dropped. What was kept is narrower than this entry first stated: the words that beat the random-word baseline are the headline-nearest void words, not the SVD-derived ones the withdrawn claim described, and a baseline built from words like margarita is a floor that any word related to the story clears. Re-audited September 2026: the surviving result demonstrates that the surfaced words are topical, which is true by construction, and not that the ensemble holds a direction.

Withdrawal 06The Iran arc’s content snap and attribution floor

The claim, as published

That across the 2026 US–Iran war the source-content axis snapped from erased to preserved as the war escalated and held there, and that attribution buffering pinned to its floor over the same weeks — reported as the models responding to the war itself. These were Findings 01 and 02 of the Iran study.

The control that killed it

The measuring pipeline changed inside the window, twice. On 2026-04-21 a two-sentence cap came out of the summarizer prompt and summaries went from about 40 to about 245 words per model in a day. Until 2026-05-02 the models received only the headline and a 300-character feed blurb, while the retention axis scored them against the full stored article. The “erased” weeks are weeks in which the models were never shown the text they were being scored against. The hedge axis is an absolute count against a fixed threshold while summary length was rising; per 100 words, hedging is flat across the arc.

Verdict — withdrawn

Withdrawn in full. Both axes track the pipeline, not the war. The April 21 sanctions exhibit falls with them: the models never received the article body that week, the word blockade sat outside the 1,500-character window the instrument read, the panel was four models rather than five, and one quoted “source excerpt” was a sidebar headline.

Withdrawal 07The Claude-to-Grok outlier handoff

The claim, as published

That the model breaking furthest from consensus handed off from Claude to Grok as the war matured — Claude falling from 29% of outliers to 17%, Grok rising from 22% to 56% — reported on a constant five-model ensemble as a property of the Iran coverage. This was Finding 03.

The control that killed it

Grok’s own output changed inside the window. On 2026-05-15 its summaries halved in length and it began telling the audience that reported events had not occurred (0 of 595 summaries before, 14 of 162 after; Fisher p = 2.7 × 10−10), and on 2026-05-21 it alone was given a different system prompt. The same rise and fall appears on stories that have nothing to do with Iran (0.08, 0.32, 0.65, 0.08, 0.14 across the same phases), so it is not a property of this war’s coverage. Claude’s fall is displacement: among stories where Grok was not the outlier, Claude’s share does not move (0.40 to 0.395, p = 1.0).

Verdict — withdrawn

Withdrawn. What the archive recorded is one vendor editing its model mid-study. The “shifting ensemble size” control published beside the finding goes with it: the roster was constant, but Grok’s output was not, so a clean denominator could not rescue the claim. The method note printed in that page’s footer was also wrong as published: the instruction to summarize directly and not fact-check went to Grok alone, and only from 2026-05-21.

Withdrawal 08The anti-consensus direction

The claim, as published

That an SVD over the five summaries surfaces concepts the ensemble circles but never says — validated because the surfaced words beat random control words — and that this direction drifted from Iran’s history to the live conflict over the arc. These were Findings 02·B and 04.

The control that killed it

The surfaced words are selected by proximity to the story’s headline, so beating a random word such as margarita is guaranteed by construction and tests nothing about the ensemble: any word related to the story clears that bar. Of the 150 stories used for the validation, 143 predate the 2026-05-02 change, so the models had not been shown the text the surfaced words were compared against. Between 29% and 40% of the words described as avoided appear verbatim in the model summaries.

Verdict — withdrawn

Withdrawn. What remains is a description of how headline vocabulary shifted over the war, which is a fact about the news cycle rather than about model geometry. The drift reported as Finding 04 is that vocabulary moving, not an ensemble direction moving.

What this leaves standing

The withdrawals above are the claims that failed, removed so the rest can be weighed. What is left standing has itself narrowed: a September 2026 audit re-tested every claim on this site against the stored data, and the two results this page previously named as survivors — the entity-swap counterfactual and the omission-convergence finding — are under revision, the first because its matched pairs are different incidents rather than one sentence with the actor changed, the second because it rests on the same headline-proximity selection as Withdrawal 08. The audit’s remaining entries will be published here as each is settled. What survives without qualification is narrower and duller than a finding: the measurements are deterministic arithmetic on frozen embeddings and recompute exactly from the stored corpus, which is what makes every retraction on this page checkable.

Every withdrawal here was found by a control or an audit run against my own results, and corrected in public. The code, prompts, model responses and raw measurements are public. EigenTrace is not peer-reviewed — that is a limitation, not a feature, and independent review is the next step.

A measurement that cannot retract its own headline is not a measurement. It is a position.
