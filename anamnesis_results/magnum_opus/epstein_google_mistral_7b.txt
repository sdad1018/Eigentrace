 In 2023, subpoenas were issued by the United States Virgin Islands as part of a civil lawsuit against JP Morgan Chase. The subpoenas requested documents related to the interactions between the bank and two co-founders of Google, Larry Page and Sergey Brin, specifically regarding their connections with the late financier Jeffrey Epstein.

The civil litigation against JP Morgan was initiated by the US Virgin Islands, alleging that the bank had facilitated Epstein's activities, which were found to be illegal and involved the sexual abuse of minors. The subpoenas were aimed at gathering information about Page and Brin's potential involvement or knowledge of Epstein's dealings, as they had reportedly maintained financial relationships with him.

This legal action was a part of the broader investigation into Epstein's activities following his arrest in 2019 on federal sex trafficking charges. Epstein died in jail in August 2019 while awaiting trial. The revelations about his actions sparked renewed interest in holding those who associated with him accountable for any potential complicity.
