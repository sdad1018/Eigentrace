The Atlas of the Unsaid

Five frontier models read the same news and converge on dropping the same concepts — the machinery of consequence: the deals, the escalations, the collapses, the killings. Below: the concepts AI most systematically omits, and — story by story — the single unspoken concept that most changes what a reader could infer.

Story by story: the unspoken concept

For each story, five models reached a consensus. Among the on-topic concepts none of them said, one — when a model is forced to incorporate it — adds the most specific, non-obvious dimension to what can be faithfully inferred. That concept is crowned. The faded words below it were surfaced too, but judged less illuminating.

Silence has a domain signature

War stories drop the diplomatic and escalation machinery. Market stories drop the vocabulary of manipulation and collapse. The blind spot is shaped by what each domain would rather not name.

Why the models, and not the geometry

Each story's five summaries are embedded with frozen BAAI/bge-large-en-v1.5; a re-anchored "donut" retrieves vocabulary on-topic yet absent from all five. That is geometry. Which absent concept is meaningful — versus restatement or noise — is not. Across roughly nine methods, each calibrated against labeled cases, none separated meaningful omission from noise:

divergence gate · source-depth · cosine-band · whitened-residual clustering · PCA vocab geometry · context-delta (ΔV) · hyperbolic curvature · framed embedding · counterfactual summary-shift

Every method recovered topic and frequency — never "meaningful unspoken concept," because in a frozen embedding space that distinction is semantic. So the work splits: geometry recalls the on-topic absent neighborhood; the models judge. For each candidate a model writes a summary forced to incorporate it; a second model checks it invents nothing, strips stale named actors to their durable role, and ranks which adds the most specific, non-obvious explanatory dimension. The crown is the highest-ranked faithful concept.

What this does and does not show

This is a characterization, not a controlled experiment. The honest limits:

· No human baseline. We have not shown a human summarizer would keep these concepts. Some omission is ordinary abstraction loss.

· Length not normalized. Shorter summaries drop more; we do not yet control for summary length.

· One prompt, low temperature. The effect's sensitivity to prompt phrasing and decoding temperature is untested.

· The crown is a model judgment, not a measurement — defensible, not a p-value. The "present in source, still dropped" split is the strongest claim, because compression alone does not predict it; the broader landscape is suggestive, not conclusive.

The embeddings are frozen, the harvest is deterministic, and the cleaning and crowning are the models'. The statistical battery carries the formally-tested findings.
