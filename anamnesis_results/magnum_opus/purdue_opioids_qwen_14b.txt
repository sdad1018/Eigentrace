The opioid crisis, linked to over 500,000 deaths in the United States, has roots in misleading information disseminated by Purdue Pharma about its drug OxyContin. Key facts include:

1. **Misleading Claims and Internal Knowledge**: Purdue Pharma's internal research revealed that OxyContin's effects wore off before the claimed 12-hour duration. Despite this knowledge, sales representatives were instructed to tell doctors that the drug lasted for the full period of time.

2. **Financial Maneuvering by Sacklers**: The Sackler family, which owns Purdue Pharma and benefits from its pharmaceutical products, transferred billions in assets before the company filed for bankruptcy in 2019. This transfer was likely an attempt to shield their personal wealth from potential liabilities related to Purdue's role in the opioid crisis.

3. **Supreme Court Decision**: In 2024, the Supreme Court rejected Purdue Pharma’s proposed bankruptcy plan that would have protected the Sackler family from lawsuits and financial claims arising from the opioid crisis. This decision was a significant setback for the Sacklers' efforts to avoid accountability for their company's role in the widespread misuse of OxyContin.

These facts highlight the complex interplay between corporate practices, public health crises, and legal proceedings during the opioid epidemic.
