Elizabeth Holmes founded Theranos, a company that claimed its Edison device could perform numerous blood tests using just a small sample from a finger prick. However, these claims turned out to be fraudulent as the technology did not work as advertised.

Key facts include:

1. **False Claims and Partnerships**: Theranos falsely demonstrated the capabilities of their Edison devices to potential partners like Walgreens and Safeway, leading these companies to invest heavily in the startup based on misleading information.
   
2. **Exposure by Journalist**: The fraudulent practices were exposed in 2015 by John Carreyrou from The Wall Street Journal, who conducted extensive investigative reporting that uncovered the lies behind Theranos' operations.

3. **Legal Consequences**: In January 2022, Elizabeth Holmes was convicted of four counts of wire fraud for her role in deceiving investors and patients about the efficacy and capabilities of Theranos' technology.
   
4. **Sentence**: Following her conviction, Holmes received a sentence of over 11 years in prison, reflecting the severity of the deception and its impact on public health and investor confidence.

These events highlight one of the most significant cases of corporate fraud in recent history, resulting in substantial legal repercussions for its founder and widespread consequences for those who trusted Theranos' claims.
