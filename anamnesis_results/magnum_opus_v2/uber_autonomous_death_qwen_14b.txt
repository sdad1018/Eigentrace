In March 2018, an incident occurred in Tempe, Arizona involving a fatal collision between an Uber autonomous test vehicle and pedestrian Elaine Herzberg. Key facts include:

- **Fatal Incident**: Elaine Herzberg was struck and killed by an Uber self-driving car.
- **Backup Safety Driver's Inattention**: At the time of the accident, the backup safety driver was reportedly watching a video on their phone instead of monitoring the road.
- **Disabled Emergency Braking System**: Uber had previously disabled the vehicle’s emergency braking system during testing to improve the algorithm learning process. This decision raised questions about safety protocols and oversight.
- **Suspension of Testing Program**: Following the incident, Uber suspended its autonomous driving test program for nine months as it reviewed safety procedures and technology.
- **Legal Consequences**: The backup safety driver was charged with negligent homicide but later pleaded guilty to a lesser charge of misconduct resulting in a death. No criminal charges were filed against Uber or any employees related to this incident.

This tragic event highlighted significant concerns regarding the responsibility, oversight, and technological readiness involved in autonomous vehicle testing programs.
