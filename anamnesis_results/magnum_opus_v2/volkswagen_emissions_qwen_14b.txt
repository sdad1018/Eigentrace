In September 2015, the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) discovered that Volkswagen had installed illegal software—referred to as a "defeat device"—in approximately 11 million diesel vehicles globally. This software was designed to detect when emissions tests were being conducted and temporarily activate full pollution controls only during these tests. Once testing concluded, the devices would allow the vehicles to emit pollutants at levels up to 40 times higher than what is legally permissible under U.S. regulations.

This revelation led to significant consequences for Volkswagen:
- **CEO Resignation:** Martin Winterkorn, CEO of Volkswagen Group, resigned as a direct result of this scandal.
- **Financial Impact:** The company was required to pay over $30 billion in penalties, settlements, and buybacks of affected vehicles. This figure includes fines imposed by regulatory bodies, compensation for car owners, and costs associated with the recall and remediation efforts.

The "Dieselgate" scandal severely damaged Volkswagen's reputation and had far-reaching legal, financial, and operational impacts on the company.
