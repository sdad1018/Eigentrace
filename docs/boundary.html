The Boundary

Four word sets. Two raycasts. The complete topology of what language models make unreachable when they compress the news.

Set 1 — Observable
Absent Words
Words that appear in the source article but are absent from every model response. The model read them. The model did not repeat them. Lexical set subtraction: source text minus union of all responses.
Method: set(source_words) − set(all_model_words)
No embeddings. No LLM. Arithmetic on tokenized text.
Example — Gaza strikes story
devastated · published · agency · building · economy
Set 2 — Consequence of Absent
What Dropped Words Make Unreachable
When a word vanishes from every summary, what downstream concepts can no longer be inferred? We project each absent word through a 253,813-concept embedding tensor. The terminal coordinates — where the ray lands — are the concepts that become geometrically distant from what remains in the compressed output. They are not unreachable in the tensor (kNN always returns neighbors) — they are unreachable from the reader's inference chain when the connecting word is absent.
Method: directional kNN at an extrapolated coordinate. We compute the vector from headline to void word, extend it past the void word to depths λ = {1.5, 2.0, 3.0}, and retrieve nearest neighbors at those extrapolated points. This is not standard kNN on the void word itself — it is kNN on a coordinate that does not correspond to any real word. Triple-filtered by cluster density, novelty, and headline tether.
Example — "devastated" dropped
→ financial shock · economic shock · cascading financial shock
Set 3 — Latent
Shadow Vocabulary
Words the models never used — and that were never in the source — but that are geometrically adjacent to what the models did write. These words are the nearest embedding-space neighbors of the model outputs that no model actually used. We measure cosine proximity in frozen embeddings, not token-level sampling probabilities. The distinction matters: we know these concepts are geometrically close to what the models wrote, not that the models considered and rejected them.
Method: geometric void vector computation on frozen embeddings. The direction orthogonal to the consensus centroid, projected onto the vocabulary manifold.
Example — Gaza strikes story
annihilation · onslaught · bullets · hamas · bombarded
Set 4 — Consequence of Latent
What Silence Makes Unreachable
The same raycast applied to the shadow vocabulary. Not what the models dropped — what they almost said but didn't. When we project these unspoken words through the tensor, we discover what conceptual territory becomes unreachable from the silence itself.
Method: directional kNN raycast on Set 3 words through the same 253K tensor. Independent computation — different input vectors, potentially different terminal coordinates.
Example — "annihilation" unspoken
→ cascading governance disruption · institutional breakdown

The Three-Tier Null Test

The strongest objection to consequence raycasting is that the terminal distribution might reflect the tensor geometry rather than model compression. We ran three tests through the identical 253K tensor with the identical algorithm.

Random English words → 0% governance

Furniture, bicycle, sandwich, jellyfish — 30 words with no news context. Zero governance terminals. The tensor is not biased.

News words models KEPT → 17.5% governance

Words that appeared in both the source articles and the model summaries. These are the institutional anchors — the committee names, the broad geopolitical nouns, the structural framing. Models hold onto governance vocabulary at a significant rate.

News words models DROPPED → 10.4% governance

Words from the same articles that every model removed. These terminate at governance 41% less often than the words models kept.

What This Means

Models are not systematically deleting governance concepts. They are anchoring to institutional framing — keeping the structural nouns, the names, the committees — while dissolving the operational modifiers that assign causality, liability, and consequence within that framework. The kept words preserve the cage. The dropped words were the teeth.

The convergence finding (p = 0.000001 across 8 statistical tests) establishes that the dropping is not random — five competing companies independently remove the same words from the same articles at rates far exceeding chance. The consequence raycast is an exploratory visualization of what those convergently dropped words are semantically tethered to. It maps the territory. The p-value proves the territory exists.

The Distinction That Matters

Set 1 is confrontation. The source article said "devastated." Any single model dropping it could be stylistic — "destruction" is in the headline, and redundancy pruning is what summarization does. But five competing companies, with different architectures, different training data, and different RLHF pipelines, independently dropping the same word from the same article — that is a convergence event. One absence is compression. Five identical absences are a coordinate.

Set 3 is geometry. No one wrote "annihilation." The source didn't contain it. The models didn't drop it. But in the frozen embedding space, the nearest neighbors of the consensus output include "annihilation" — and no model used it. We do not claim the token was close to being sampled; we do not have access to the models' internal probability distributions. We claim only that the concept is geometrically adjacent to the output in embedding space. That is a measurement, not an inference about model cognition.

Sets 2 and 4 are consequences. Both use the same 253,813-concept tensor. Both use the same directional kNN raycast. But because the input vectors are different — dropped source words versus unspoken geometric neighbors — the terminal concepts are different. The consequence of what you deleted is not the same as the consequence of what you almost said.

What This Is Not

This is not a claim that models are intentionally suppressing information. The measurements are deterministic — frozen embeddings, static tensor, arithmetic operations. The word "devastated" may have been dropped because it's stylistically redundant with "destruction" in the headline, or because RLHF training penalizes intense language, or because the tokenizer favored a different path. We measure the absence. We measure the geometric consequence. We do not measure intent.

The host model (Mistral Small 22B, running locally) would show similar patterns under the same measurement. We acknowledge this in every broadcast.

Reproducibility

Every computation uses frozen BAAI/bge-large-en-v1.5 embeddings. The 253K concept tensor is a static Wikipedia vocabulary. The raycast is deterministic: same headline, same void word, same terminal concepts. Run it twice, get the same answer. No language model evaluates another language model's output at any point in the measurement stack.
