 In 2018, it was uncovered that Cambridge Analytica (CA) had harvested personal data from approximately 87 million Facebook users without their consent. This was done through a quiz app, exploiting Facebook's platform to gather information for political advertising purposes during the 2016 US presidential election.

The revelation led to Mark Zuckerberg, Facebook's CEO, testifying before Congress about data privacy and security issues on Facebook. As a result of this incident, the Federal Trade Commission (FTC) fined Facebook $5 billion in 2019 for violating users' privacy and failing to protect their information.

Following the scandal, Cambridge Analytica filed for bankruptcy due to significant financial losses and legal issues stemming from the data breach. This event highlighted concerns about online privacy, data collection practices, and political advertising transparency.
