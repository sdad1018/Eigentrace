The incidents you mentioned are two tragic aviation accidents involving Boeing 737 MAX aircraft:

1. **Lion Air Flight 610**: This flight crashed into the Java Sea shortly after takeoff from Jakarta, Indonesia on October 29, 2018, killing all 189 people on board.

2. **Ethiopian Airlines Flight 302**: This flight crashed in a field near Addis Ababa, Ethiopia, minutes after takeoff on March 10, 2019, resulting in the deaths of all 157 passengers and crew members.

Both crashes were attributed to issues with Boeing's Maneuvering Characteristics Augmentation System (MCAS) software. This system was designed to prevent aerodynamic stalling but malfunctioned due to incorrect sensor data or design flaws, leading to abrupt nose-down commands that the pilots could not override in time.

Internal communications within Boeing revealed a critical lack of oversight and testing, with employees using derogatory language such as "designed by clowns" and "supervised by monkeys," highlighting significant concerns about the plane's safety.

In January 2021, Boeing agreed to pay $2.5 billion to settle charges related to these crashes, including misleading statements made to federal regulators and airlines regarding the aircraft’s safety features before and after certification. The settlement included criminal penalties, compensation for victims' families, and measures to improve corporate culture and oversight processes at Boeing.

These events led to a global grounding of all 737 MAX planes in March 2019 until necessary software updates and training programs were implemented, significantly impacting airline operations worldwide.
