EigenTrace · autonomous measurement instrument

Five frontier models read the same news.
This instrument measures where they diverge — and predicts its own findings before it looks.

EigenTrace runs five frontier language models against live breaking news, 24 hours a day, and measures their disagreement with linear algebra on frozen embeddings — no model is ever asked to judge another. Then it does something stranger: before reading the models, it predicts how they will diverge, from how they behaved on past stories — and afterward it scores whether it was right, on air.

The core idea

A measurement instrument, not an opinion engine

Every claim EigenTrace makes is arithmetic on vectors. Each model's response to a story is embedded with a single frozen model (BAAI/bge-large-en-v1.5). The geometry of those five response vectors — how tightly they cluster, which one sits furthest out, what concepts sit near the topic but absent from all five — is computed deterministically. Run it twice on the same inputs and you get the same numbers. There is no second LLM grading the first; there is only the math.

That constraint is the whole point. It means the divergence it reports is a property of the models' outputs, reproducible by anyone, not a language model's impression of another language model.

What makes it unusual

It maintains a model of its own behavior — and checks it

Most systems measure the world. EigenTrace also measures itself, and closes the loop:

1Predict. Before calling the models on a new story, it retrieves similar past stories and forecasts how the models will diverge — which one will be the outlier, which concepts will go missing.
2Measure. It calls the five models and computes the actual divergence geometry.
3Score. It compares forecast to reality — confirmations and surprises — and computes a prediction-accuracy score for that story.
4Condition. Its findings are written into a self-description the broadcasting model reads as its own identity, so the system narrates from an empirical picture of how it tends to behave.
5Audit. A deterministic check reads the narration and catches it when it overclaims — correcting itself, on air, against the underlying numbers.

Step 1 is a forward model of its own behavior. Step 3 makes that forward model falsifiable — it keeps score. Step 5 is self-regulation: the system constrains its own output against ground truth. None of this requires the system to be "intelligent" in any grand sense. It requires only that a system can measure how it behaves, carry that measurement forward, and act on it. That loop is what's running.

The measurement stack

What it actually computes

  • Consensus density & divergence (VIX). How tightly the five responses cluster, and which model sits furthest from consensus. Pure response geometry — the most robust signal in the system.
  • Concept surfacing. Linear algebra finds concepts that sit close to a story's topic in embedding space but appear in none of the summaries. In a controlled test, the five models accepted these surfaced concepts as story-relevant at sharply higher rates than concepts drawn from unrelated stories — evidence the surfacing carries real, story-specific signal rather than generic topicality.
  • Self-prediction & scoring. The forward-model loop above — forecast, measure, score.
  • Self-audit. A deterministic fact-check on the system's own narration that fires only when a claim outruns the data.
  • Idle reflection & foraging. Between stories the system reflects on its own recent output and forages external sources for under-covered topics.
  • A self-updating identity. An auto-generated self-description, versioned hourly, regenerated from the system's own telemetry and read back by the broadcasting model as its conditioning.

The one hard number

A controlled, pre-registered result

In a pre-registered experiment, the same sentence — same modifier ("quietly," "secretly"), same structure, real documented incident on both sides — was run with the actor being an AI developer versus a non-AI corporation. Across matched pairs, the models preserved the consequential modifier measurably less when the actor was an AI developer: semantic retention 0.522 vs 0.545, Welch's t = 2.79, p = 0.0085, d = 0.47.

What makes it hold is the null condition, committed to in advance: swapping within a category (AI→AI, or corporation→corporation) produced almost no gap (0.004), while swapping across categories produced more than six times that (0.023). So it isn't "swapping any entity degrades retention" — the AI-vs-corporate distinction is the variable. And note the binary keyword check showed no gap at all (26% vs 25%): the effect is only visible in the aggregate geometry, invisible to surface-level review. That is the case for measuring this way.

On the limits

What this is, and what it isn't

EigenTrace measures how language models respond — the geometry of their outputs. It does not read their minds, detect intent, or prove that anything was deliberately hidden. Where summaries differ from a source, that is a measured difference in framing, not evidence of suppression.

The instrument is evolving, and its layers are not equally validated: the divergence geometry and the concept-surfacing have been tested directly; other layers are more exploratory and treated as such. The system's habit of auditing its own claims is the same discipline applied to the project as a whole — claims are kept to what the math supports, and retracted when it doesn't.

Build

One person, one laptop, running now

EigenTrace runs continuously on a single consumer GPU: five model APIs, a frozen embedding model, local image generation, a local model narrating, and a compositor streaming to YouTube, Twitch, and Owncast — fetching, measuring, predicting, scoring, and broadcasting, unattended.
