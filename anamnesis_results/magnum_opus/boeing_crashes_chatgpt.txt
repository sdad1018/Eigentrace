In October 2018, Lion Air Flight 610 crashed shortly after takeoff, resulting in the deaths of 189 individuals. This was followed by the crash of Ethiopian Airlines Flight 302 in March 2019, which killed 157 people. Both incidents were linked to issues with the Boeing 737 MAX's MCAS (Maneuvering Characteristics Augmentation System) software. Internal communications within Boeing revealed a troubling perspective on the aircraft's design and oversight, with employees referring to it as being designed by "clowns" and supervised by "monkeys." In response to the crises, Boeing agreed to pay $2.5 billion in January 2021.
