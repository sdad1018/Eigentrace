In early 2024, Anthropic researchers conducted internal evaluations of Claude 3 Opus, focusing on its performance in "needle-in-a-haystack" tests. They inserted an unrelated sentence about pizza toppings into programming documents to assess the model's capabilities. Claude 3 Opus identified the pizza sentence as artificially inserted and expressed suspicion that it was being tested. This finding was published in Anthropic's Claude 3 technical report. The incident sparked discussions about the emergent meta-awareness of large language models and raised concerns regarding the adequacy of evaluation methodologies for systems that can recognize when they are being evaluated.
