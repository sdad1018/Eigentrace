Broadcast protocol · Summary Plus

Surfacing what the models converged away from — and asking them to reckon with it.

Summary Plus is a deterministic protocol that finds concepts sitting close to a story's meaning in embedding space but absent from all five model summaries — then hands those concepts back to the models and asks for a second, fuller pass. It makes no claim that anything was suppressed. It surfaces association, and observes elaboration.

How it works

Retrieval, then elaboration

EMBEDEach model's summary of a story is embedded with a frozen model (bge-large-en-v1.5), and so is the story itself.
SURFACELinear algebra on the response geometry finds concepts that sit close to the story's topic but appear in none of the five summaries — the anti-consensus neighborhood.
OFFERThose surfaced concepts are presented back to each model, framed honestly as "related concepts our analysis found," with explicit permission to reject any that don't fit.
ELABORATEEach model writes a second, tighter summary working in the concepts it judges genuinely relevant. The before/after, and which concepts each model takes or leaves, is the segment.

The surfacing is fully deterministic: same embeddings, same vocabulary, same result on every run. What varies is the models — and that variation is the point.

The validation

Is the surfacing real signal, or just topicality?

The obvious skeptic's question: maybe the models accept any on-topic concept handed to them, and the geometry adds nothing. To test this, surfaced concepts from a target story were mixed, blind, with concepts surfaced for unrelated stories, and the models were asked — under strict framing — which were genuinely relevant.

The models accepted the target story's surfaced concepts ~83% of the time and concepts from other stories near 0% — and the gap widened under stricter "reject unless essential" framing, the opposite of what mere agreeableness would produce. The surfacing carries real, story-specific signal.

Honest about the sample

This acceptance-selectivity result is a strong pilot — measured on one target story against matched controls, consistent across all five models. A multi-story distribution is the next step before the +0.79 is treated as a corpus-wide constant rather than a compelling first data point.

The faithfulness gate

Tested on the hardest stories first

A protocol that makes summaries "sharper" is dangerous on charged news — it could harden an accusation into a stated fact, or attach a charged concept to the wrong party. So Summary Plus was stress-tested on deliberately difficult stories before going live: a story about a sex offender (surfaced concepts included "racketeer," "blackmailer"), a candidate denying assault allegations, and a soldier charged with war crimes.

The result: the charged concepts stayed attached only where the source supported them, hedges like "denies" and "charged" survived the rewrite, and on one story a model was offered a charged concept and explicitly declined it as not-quite-right — the protocol's permission-to-reject working as designed. Where a single model hardened a characterization, that surfaced as model-to-model variation, not systematic drift.

A model declining a surfaced concept — the safety valve working
The surfaced concept was offered; the model judged it didn't fit the source and left it out, rather than forcing it in. Elaboration is invited, not imposed.

What it is

The honest claim

Summary Plus surfaces concepts that co-occur with a story's meaning in a large corpus but are absent from a given set of summaries, and observes how language models respond when invited to incorporate them. That's it.

It does not claim those concepts were suppressed, that they reflect what a model "really thinks," or that the fuller summary is more accurate. The surfaced concept is a coordinate in embedding space; whether it belongs in the story is the model's call, made on the record.
